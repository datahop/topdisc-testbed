# TopDisc on AWS: run `aws-five-1k`

## Summary

- **Fleet.** 858 on-demand instances in four regions, the most the per-region vCPU quotas allow. Every node ran to the end and every trace arrived. No host dropped a packet. Node CPU was 0 % at the median and 6 % at the peak.
- **Registration.** Every advertiser reached 30 to 41 registrars, 34 at the median. The quoted wait was 16 s at the median. A registration took about a minute. Ads held peaked at 402 per registrar against a cache of 5 000.
- **Search.** Every searcher found every registrant of its topic. Time to recall grows with topic size. The 392-member topic reached 99 % in 33 s at the median, the 69-member topic in 6 s.
- **Load.** The busiest registrar answered 3.9 times the median, 7 496 queries against 1 902. Traffic per node was 13 MB at the median and 47 MB at the most. Per-node load is the same as in the 540-node run of the same morning once normalised by run length.
- **Re-walk.** On the two large topics searchers kept walking their tables after they had found everything, 11 passes on topic 0 with a third of the results filtered. This loop sets the registrar load on large topics over a long run.

## Scenario

858 real TopDisc nodes on AWS, one per on-demand t4g.nano instance, over us-east-1, us-west-2, eu-central-1 and ap-southeast-1. Five Zipf topics. Every node registers one topic and searches it continuously, with no target count and the connection model off, for 51 minutes. Nodes start over 3 minutes, registration starts at +1 min, search at +9 min, and the run stops at +60 min. The searcher is the adaptive search distance with yield floor 4. The fork is `github.com/datahop/go-ethereum@v1.17.2-testbed.9`. There is no churn. Protocol defaults apply: ad lifetime 15 min, cache 5 000 ads, 10 buckets, K_register 5, K_lookup 16.
